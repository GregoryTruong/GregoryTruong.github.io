Exit 3Car Depreciation AnalyzerÔ tô
Project · Machine learningPython · pandas · scikit-learn · matplotlib · pytest

When should you actually buy that car?

An end-to-end pipeline that ingests, cleans and merges used-car listings from two sources, then trains a gradient boosting model with monotonic constraints on age and mileage.

16,000+listings merged and cleaned
5.6%median error on price
R² 0.93on 3,294 held-out cars
~½yearly cost when bought at the best age, for 5 of 6 models

Every public stock gets a report card.

ABCDF

A Streamlit dashboard that grades any ticker on growth, profitability, valuation and risk by scoring 10 financial metrics against S&P 500 benchmarks, then explains the grade in plain English.

Why I built it: I wanted to open a TFSA and stop letting my money sit in a savings account and not grow. Before I put real money in, I wanted a quick way to tell a solid company from a risky one.

Exit 6Under the hoodToán học
How this site works

The math behind the drive

There's no 3D engine here. Every frame is drawn flat on a canvas from five equations.

Perspective
x′ = W/2 + f (x − c) / z
Divide every point by its distance z. Far things shrink toward the horizon. That one division is the whole 3D effect.
Curves
Δx(z) = 0.0022 κ z2
A circle of radius R drifts sideways by about z2/2R (a second-order Taylor expansion). Shift each slice of road by κz2 and it bends.
Steering
κ(s) = 0.9 sin(s/110) + 0.25 sin(φ2s/110)
Two waves whose frequencies differ by a factor of φ2, the golden ratio squared. That ratio is irrational, so the road is quasi-periodic: the same stretch never comes back twice.
Towers
hn = 14 + 81 {nφ}
Street-side tower heights are the fractional parts of nφ (on a phone the 81 shrinks to 46). By Weyl's equidistribution theorem they spread evenly between short and tall, with no clumps of identical towers.
Fog
α(z) = min(1, 1.12 (z/280)0.65)
A power law blends each colour into the smog, so the city fades out slowly instead of hitting a wall.
